# Gurukul: phase 2 curriculum (Class 5: Ganita, Vijnana, Loka)

> Status: draft for review by Class 5 maths, science and social studies teachers (2026-10-11).
> **All non-English names and strings are drafts** and need review by a native-speaking teacher for each language.
> Source syllabus: the child's Class 5 books photographed in [`docs/topics/`](../topics/) (maths syllabus with seven themes; science contents, 12 chapters; social studies contents, 18 chapters in seven themes). Chapter titles below are copied from those pages; the content of each knot must be checked against the chapter itself.

## 1. How to read this

- A **Shala** is a subject: **Ganita** (mathematics), **Vijnana** (science), **Loka** (social studies).
- A **thread** (sutra) is a strand of a Shala, such as *Sankhya* (numbers).
- A **knot** (ID like `N1`) is one concept: one lesson of 10–15 minutes with the anatomy in [EXPERIENCE.md §3](EXPERIENCE.md#3-lesson-anatomy-in-detail), including **Bodha** (§3.2).
- Status: **P1** = playable since phase 1 (now with Bodha) · **P2** = new playable lesson in the phase 2 prototype · **Planned** = in the curriculum, not yet in the prototype.
- **Misconception** is the specific wrong idea the knot surfaces and fixes. Bodha names it; Spot the mistake makes the child find it.

## 7. The nine new playable lessons (P2)

Each entry gives the Bodha cards in brief. The exact on-screen text is in the prototype's English strings (`design/prototype/js/i18n/en-*.js`).

### N1 · Lakh and crore · **P2** (`#place`, Place-value houses)
- **Goal:** read and write numbers up to crores; understand that each place is worth ten of the place on its right; use Indian (lakh, crore) and international (thousand, million) commas.
- **Misconception:** writing "two lakh five thousand" as 2,5000 (digits put side by side, not into places); thinking 1 lakh equals 1 million.
- **Prashna:** a stadium holds 1,00,000 people. Is that more than, less than or the same as one million? (Less: a million is ten lakh.)
- **Bodha:** what (the same ten digits make every number; position gives value) · idea (ten in one place bundle into one in the next) · why (commas group places so we can read them; India groups 3 then 2s) · example (read 12,34,567 in four steps) · where (India's population, about 140 crore; prices in lakh) · sutra: *"Each place is worth ten of the place on its right."*
- **Lenses:** bead houses → digits in a place chart → Indian and international commas → expanded form.
- **Workbench:** (1) watch: add one bead to 99,999, every house bundles, 1,00,000; (2) build 3,05,200; (3) write 4,50,000 the international way.
- **Spot the mistake:** Asha writes two lakh five thousand as 2,5000.
- **Explain back:** "Each place is worth ___ times the place on its right. In India, after the thousands, commas come every ___ digits."

### N3 · Equal parts · **P2** (`#fractions`, Folding strips)
- **Goal:** a fraction names equal parts of one whole; the denominator counts the parts and the numerator counts those taken; equivalent fractions; comparing unit fractions.
- **Misconception:** "1/3 is bigger than 1/2 because 3 is bigger than 2"; parts need not be equal.
- **Prashna:** Ravi eats 1/2 of a roti; Mini eats 2/4 of the same size roti. Who ate more? (The same.)
- **Bodha:** what · idea (cut into equal parts, count the parts taken) · why (parts must be equal) · example (1/2 → 2/4 → 4/8 by folding again) · where (half a litre of milk, quarter kilo of tomatoes, half an hour) · sutra: *"Cut the whole into equal parts: the bottom number counts the parts, the top counts the ones you take."*
- **Lenses:** folded paper strip → shaded parts → the fraction symbol → a line showing equal lengths.
- **Workbench:** (1) watch: 1/2 and 2/4 line up; (2) make 3/4 and an equal fraction in eighths (6/8); (3) which is bigger, 2/3 or 3/4?
- **Spot the mistake:** Kiran says 1/3 is bigger than 1/2.
- **Explain back:** "The bottom number tells how many ___ parts the whole is cut into. When I fold each part in two, the fraction looks different but the amount stays ___."

### U1 · Rupees and paise · **P2** (`#money`, The rupee grid)
- **Goal:** one rupee is 100 paise; the first digit after the decimal point counts tenths (10 paise), the second counts hundredths (1 paise); compare decimals by place, not by length.
- **Misconception:** "₹0.45 is more than ₹0.5 because 45 is bigger than 5" (longer decimal is bigger).
- **Prashna:** a pencil costs ₹4.5 and an eraser ₹4.25. Which costs more? (The pencil: ₹4.50.)
- **Bodha:** what (the dot separates whole rupees from parts of a rupee) · idea (a rupee as 10 strips of 10 paise squares) · why (place value continues: each place is a tenth of the one on its left) · example (₹3.75 = 3 rupees + 7 tenths + 5 hundredths = 375 paise) · where (bills, fuel prices, cricket run rates) · sutra: *"After the dot, each place is a tenth of the place on its left."*
- **Lenses:** rupee grid → ₹ amount → paise → place chart (rupees . tenths hundredths).
- **Workbench:** (1) watch: ₹1.25 built from one rupee, two strips and five squares; (2) build ₹2.50; (3) compare ₹0.5 and ₹0.45 side by side.
- **Spot the mistake:** Neha says ₹0.45 is more than ₹0.5.
- **Explain back:** "The first digit after the dot counts ___ of a rupee. ₹0.5 is the same as ___ paise."
- **Note:** 1 paise and 10 paise coins are no longer in everyday use; the grid uses paise as a unit of measure, as the textbook does, not as coins.

### B3 · Journey of a roti · **P2** (`#digest`, digestion path)
- **Goal:** digestion breaks food into pieces small enough for the blood to carry; the order of organs (mouth, food pipe, stomach, small intestine, large intestine); most food enters the blood in the small intestine; waste leaves the body.
- **Misconception:** the stomach is where food goes into the blood, or where digestion ends; food goes "straight to the stomach" without chewing doing anything. (Children's ideas about digestion: RESEARCH C21.)
- **Prashna:** in grown-ups, the small intestine is about 6 metres long. About how many times their height is that? (About four times.)
- **Bodha:** what · idea (the food path, organ by organ) · why (blood can only carry tiny pieces) · example (one bite of roti, five stops) · where (chew well; vegetables and water help; kidneys clean the blood and make urine) · sutra: *"Digestion breaks food small enough for the blood to carry; what the body cannot use, it removes."*
- **Facts used** (to be checked by a science teacher): mouth, chewing and saliva begin breaking down starch; the food pipe squeezes food down in seconds; the stomach churns and mixes for about 2–4 hours; the small intestine (about 6 m in adults) finishes digestion and absorbs most nutrients over several hours; the large intestine absorbs water over about a day; waste leaves through the anus.
- **Workbench:** (1) watch the roti travel; (2) put the organs in order; (3) tap where most food passes into the blood.
- **Spot the mistake:** Rohan says the stomach passes food into the blood.
- **Explain back:** "Food is broken down so it can pass into the ___. Most of this happens in the ___."

### K2 · The ramp test · **P2** (`#ramp`)
- **Goal:** friction is a force between surfaces that rub, acting against motion; rougher surfaces give more friction; a fair test changes one thing at a time.
- **Misconception:** friction only exists on rough things or only slows things; a test that changes two things at once can show which one mattered.
- **Prashna:** a toy car rolls off a ramp onto smooth tiles or onto a rug. Where does it go further? (Tiles.)
- **Bodha:** what · idea (tiny bumps catch on each other) · why (friction pushes against the motion) · example (same ramp, same car, tile vs sand) · where (shoe grip, oil on a cycle chain, sand on a slippery road, rubbing hands warm) · sutra: *"Rough surfaces rub more, and friction slows things down."*
- **Workbench:** (1) watch: tile and sand from the same height; (2) fair test: compare surfaces with the ramp kept the same (if the child changes the ramp too, the model says so); (3) rank the four surfaces from most friction to least.
- **Note:** the distances in the model are illustrative, labelled "pretend measurements", and the child is invited to try a real ramp at home.
- **Spot the mistake:** Tara changes the ramp height and the floor at the same time.
- **Explain back:** "Rough surfaces have ___ friction, so the car stops ___. In a fair test I change only ___ thing."

### R1 · The lamp and the stick · **P2** (`#shadow`)
- **Goal:** light travels in straight lines; a shadow forms where an opaque object blocks light, on the side away from the light; a low light makes a long shadow, a high light a short one; opaque, translucent and transparent materials.
- **Misconception:** a shadow is a reflection or a "copy" of the object; lowering the light makes a shorter shadow.
- **Prashna:** at noon, is your shadow long or short? (Short.)
- **Bodha:** what · idea (straight rays, blocked rays) · why (where the light is decides where the shadow falls and how long it is) · example (lamp low, higher, overhead) · where (morning and noon shadows, the giant sundial at Jantar Mantar, shade under a tree) · sutra: *"Light goes straight; where it is blocked, a shadow forms on the far side."*
- **Workbench:** (1) watch the lamp rise and the shadow shrink; (2) make the shadow longer than the stick; (3) which material gives the darkest shadow?
- **Model:** shadow length = stick height × horizontal distance ÷ (lamp height − stick height).
- **Spot the mistake:** Ali says a lower torch makes a shorter shadow.
- **Explain back:** "Light travels in ___ lines. When the light is higher, the shadow is ___."

### I2 · Thread of time · **P2** (`#timeline`)
- **Goal:** a timeline is a number line for years; CE counts forward from year 1 and BCE counts backward; a bigger BCE number is longer ago; there is no year zero; centuries.
- **Misconception:** 300 BCE is more recent than 200 BCE; counting across the BCE/CE join as if there were a year zero (50 BCE to 50 CE = 100 years).
- **Prashna:** which happened longer ago, something in 300 BCE or something in 200 BCE? (300 BCE.)
- **Bodha:** what · idea (BCE counts back, CE counts forward) · why (there is no year 0: 1 BCE is followed by 1 CE) · example (100 BCE to 100 CE: 99 + 1 + 99 = 199 years) · where (dates on coins and inscriptions; 1947 is in the 20th century because the first century was years 1 to 100) · sutra: *"BCE counts back, CE counts forward, and there is no year zero."*
- **Events on the thread** (approximate dates are marked "about"): Mohenjo-daro, a city of the Harappan civilisation, about 2500 BCE · Ashoka's edicts, about 250 BCE · Aryabhata writes the *Aryabhatiya*, 499 CE · the Taj Mahal is finished, about 1653 CE · India becomes independent, 1947 CE · Chandrayaan-3 lands near the Moon's south pole, 2023 CE.
- **Workbench:** (1) watch the thread unroll across the join; (2) hang the six events in order; (3) years from Ashoka's edicts (250 BCE) to Aryabhata (499 CE): 748.
- **Spot the mistake:** Dev counts 50 BCE to 50 CE as 100 years.
- **Explain back:** "On a timeline, a bigger BCE number is ___ ago. There is ___ year zero."

### E1 · Spin and travel · **P2** (`#earth`)
- **Goal:** the Earth spins on its axis about once every 24 hours, giving day and night; it travels around the Sun about once every 365¼ days; its tilted axis gives the seasons.
- **Misconception:** the Sun goes around the Earth each day; summer happens because the Earth is closer to the Sun (in fact the Earth is closest to the Sun in early January, during northern winter).
- **Prashna:** why do we have day and night? (The Earth spins.)
- **Bodha:** what · idea (spinning: the half facing the Sun has day) · why (travelling with a tilt: our half leans toward the Sun in June) · example (India through one spin: sunrise, noon, sunset, midnight) · where (the Sun rises in Arunachal Pradesh nearly two hours before Gujarat; 29 February every four years) · sutra: *"Spinning gives day and night; travelling round the Sun with a tilt gives the seasons."*
- **Workbench:** (1) watch one spin; (2) turn the Earth so it is night in India; (3) carry the Earth to where India has winter (December side: the northern half tilts away).
- **Spot the mistake:** Priya says summer is hot because the Earth is closer to the Sun.
- **Explain back:** "Day and night happen because the Earth ___. Seasons happen because the Earth's axis is ___."

### E4 · Courtyard from above · **P2** (`#map`)
- **Goal:** a map is a drawing of a place from above, made smaller by the same amount everywhere; scale, direction (north at the top, east to the right) and symbols (legend).
- **Misconception:** adding the scale instead of multiplying (3 squares at 10 m each = 13 m); maps always show things from the side.
- **Prashna:** on most maps, which direction is at the top? (North.)
- **Bodha:** what · idea (three keys: scale, direction, symbols) · why (everything shrinks by the same amount, so shapes and distances stay true) · example (gate to well: 9 squares × 5 m = 45 m) · where (phone maps, metro maps, the school atlas) · sutra: *"A map shrinks a place by the same amount everywhere; scale, direction and symbols let you read it."*
- **Workbench:** (1) watch the courtyard fold flat into a map; (2) walk from the gate to the well with N, E, S and W; (3) with 1 square = 5 m, how far did you walk?
- **Spot the mistake:** Sam measures 3 squares at 10 m each and says 13 m.
- **Explain back:** "A map shows a place from ___. The scale tells me how much ___ each part of the map stands for."
